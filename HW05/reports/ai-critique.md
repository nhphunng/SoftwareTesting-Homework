# AI Critique

The AI significantly accelerated the performance-testing workflow by generating JMeter plans, automating evidence collection, and analyzing large JTL files, but it could not independently guarantee experimental validity. Its generated skills were often tightly coupled to a specific phase and Scenario C assumptions, which reduced their reuse across other endpoint groups or similar tests. Human control therefore remained necessary at both the design and interpretation levels. I changed several high-level AI directions: I selected Scenario C instead of the preliminary Candidate A, replaced the early k6 scaffold with the class-required JMeter workflow, required automatic account provisioning after every backend reset, and selected Load Run02 as the complete Phase 6 evidence identity after the original resource CSV proved unusable. In contrast, I reviewed and accepted the numerical Load, Stress, Spike, and Endurance workload proposals without modifying their parameters; meaningful human review does not require changing a proposal when its assumptions and evidence are reasonable.

The most important corrections concerned the interpretation of measured data. Eighty active threads was the maximum tested concurrency, not a demonstrated capacity ceiling. The measured 26.23 value represented complete business flows per second, not HTTP requests per second, and 188.84 MiB was the maximum observed RSS rather than a proven memory ceiling. Similarly, the populated Load Run02 resource CSV initially appeared to be valid evidence, but inspection showed that its rows did not match the declared header and provided no attributable backend CPU or RSS measurements. This demonstrated that the existence of an artifact does not establish its validity as evidence. These failures show that AI can follow labels, file structures, and numerical patterns without independently proving run identity, units, metric attribution, or experimental scope. I therefore learned to define more reusable skill boundaries, preserve explicit human approval gates, and validate every conclusion against untouched JTL files, runner code, and evidence from the same run. AI can accelerate test engineering, but the tester remains responsible for evidence validity, experimental interpretation, and final judgment.
